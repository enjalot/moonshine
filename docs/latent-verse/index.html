Latent Verse

A workbench for studying latent spaces.

53,133 dad jokes, each a dot, placed by the similarity of their embeddings. Part 2 builds this map.

Language models learn something about the meaning of text. When they embed a sentence, they produce a list of numbers that places it at a point in a high-dimensional space. Similar texts land near each other, and related ideas form neighborhoods that no one designed.

Using 53,000 dad jokes as our specimen, we build up a set of tools for seeing that structure: projecting embeddings onto a 2D map, finding and naming groups, decomposing embeddings into interpretable features, and training a map that stays fixed from one dataset to the next.

Each article adds one piece, and each piece has a nickname we use along the way: the embedding model is the lens, UMAP the projector, the sparse autoencoder the filter, and the fixed map with its feature names the atlas. The tools are open source: Latent Scope covers Parts 1 to 3, latent-sae trains the features in Part 4 and latent-basemap the map in Part 5.
