A ten-part series

What Dimensionality Reduction Preserves

A field guide to the methods that turn high-dimensional data into something we can look at, organized by the kind of structure each one protects.

Every dimensionality reduction algorithm makes a decision about what matters in the data. PCA keeps variance, t-SNE keeps who is near whom, Mapper keeps shape, and Poincaré embeddings keep hierarchy. Choosing a method is choosing which kind of structure the picture will show.

The literature usually organizes these methods by how they are computed: eigendecompositions, gradient descent, neural networks. That suits implementers. For someone choosing a tool, it is more useful to ask what the method is trying to preserve, because that is what tells you whether it matches your data and your question.

This series is organized that way. Each article takes one family of methods built around one notion of structure, shows what the family keeps and what it discards, and runs the methods in the page on small datasets so the trade-offs can be seen. It is written for machine-learning practitioners who work with high-dimensional data and want a clearer basis for choosing and reading projections of latent spaces and embeddings.

How to read

Each article can be read on its own, but the order matters. Each family addresses something the previous one could not do, and the first article sets up the vocabulary and the measurements the others use. If the territory is new to you, read in sequence. If you are here for one method, go straight to its article.

The figures run the algorithms in your browser on a few hundred points: PCA, MDS, Isomap, t-SNE, UMAP, densMAP, Mapper, a self-organizing map, and a small autoencoder. Where a figure simplifies a method, its caption says how.

The literature behind these articles is summarized at latent-labs/research, a set of paper notes covering these methods and the areas around them (clustering, evaluation, topological data analysis, embedding tools).
